The Law of Surprise, a medieval practice dating back to humanity's earliest days, dictates that when one saves another, they are expected to offer a boon whose nature is unknown to both parties. This obligation often involves the provision of a firstborn child, conceived or born without the father's knowledge, to their savior as recompense. In the Witcher lore, this principle is exemplified through several notable instances involving Geralt of Rivia: King Roegner of Cintra, in gratitude for curing Urcheon of Erlenwald, offered his daughter Pavetta to Geralt; after curing Duny from a curse, he gifted him Pavetta and their unborn child Ciri. When Geralt saved the life of Yurga, Duny's wife had taken in the orphan girl Ciri as a war orphan. Despite often debunking its existence, Geralt named Ciri as his reward for saving Yurga's life a second time. The Law of Surprise is also connected to legendary figures like Zatret Voruta and Mad Deï, where children were given to dwarves or families in return for favors performed by the fathers. In the Price of Neutrality premium module for The Witcher, Deidre Ademeyn's character Eskel features as 'Eskel's Unexpected Child'. This tradition is rooted in Slavic mythology and biblical Judges 11:30, where Jephthah sacrifices his daughter to God for victory. The Law of Surprise significantly influences Witcher lore, intertwining destiny with the practice and suggesting dire consequences if it's not adhered to.
